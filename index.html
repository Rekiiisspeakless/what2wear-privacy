What2Wear Privacy Policy

Last updated 3 November 2026

This policy explains what the What2Wear app for Android does with your information: the weather it fetches, what it keeps on your phone, its ads, and buying Plus.

  • There's no account to make, and What2Wear has no servers of its own.
  • Your location is used to get the weather, from Open-Meteo, and to name the place you're in, through your phone's own place lookup from Google.
  • Free shows ads from Google AdMob. Plus has none and makes no ad requests.
  • We don't sell your information, and the app has no analytics or crash reporting.

Your location and the weather

If you allow it, What2Wear uses your phone's approximate location (not its precise GPS position) to show the weather where you are. You can turn this off at any time with “Use my location” in Settings, and use cities you choose instead.

To get the weather, the app sends the coordinates of the place you're looking at, or of a Trip's destination, to Open-Meteo, a free weather service. When you search for a place and your phone's own place lookup (below) finds nothing, the text you type is sent to Open-Meteo's place search instead. Open-Meteo's own terms and privacy information apply to those requests. The Home Screen Widget refreshes its weather in the background the same way, and so do the notifications you turn on: each is prepared on your phone and handed to Android to show.

To name the place you're in, such as your district or town, What2Wear asks your phone's own place lookup (Android's Geocoder), which sends your location's coordinates and the app's language to Google. Nothing else is sent with them. Naming it happens only when the app gets the weather where you are, and never when you use cities you choose instead.

When you search for a place, the text you type and the app's language are sent to Google through the same place lookup, to find matching places. When you make a Trip to a city saved by an earlier version of the app, that city's coordinates are sent through it once, to find its country. Google's privacy policy applies to these requests.

What stays on your phone

These are kept only on your phone, never sent to us:

  • your settings, such as units and how warm or cold you usually feel
  • your Saved Places and the most recent weather for them
  • the last name found for the place you're in, shown for up to 3 hours
  • your Trips, their packing lists and the weather they were made from
  • whether you own Plus, so it still works offline

They're removed when you uninstall the app or clear its data in Android's settings.

Ads

On Free, What2Wear shows one ad on its Today screen, from Google AdMob. To show and measure ads, and to prevent fraud, AdMob may collect information from your phone, such as its advertising ID, IP address, general location from that IP address, device details, and how you interact with the ad. Google explains this in How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services.

Where the law requires it (such as in the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland), the app asks for your consent with Google's consent form before showing ads, and you can change your choice at any time in Settings → Privacy → Ad choices. You can also reset or delete your advertising ID in Android's settings.

Ads are set for a general audience and aren't personalised for children. With Plus, the app makes no ad requests at all.

Buying Plus

Plus is bought through Google Play. Google handles the payment; we never see your card or payment details. The app asks Google Play whether your Google account owns Plus, so it can turn Plus on and restore it on a new phone. Google's privacy policy covers Google Play.

Children

What2Wear is for a general audience and isn't directed at children under 13.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted here with a new date.
